Activities & Campaigns

A look at the festivals, observances and humanitarian campaigns that Patanjali Yogpeeth Nepal and the Patanjali Yog Samiti carry out across the country each year — from mass yoga celebrations to earthquake relief.

Festival

International Yoga Day Celebration

अन्तर्राष्ट्रिय योग दिवस

Patanjali Yogpeeth Nepal, operating through its local body the Patanjali Yog Samiti, plays a significant and consistent role in the annual International Yoga Day (IYD) celebrations across the country. Since the UN declared IYD in 2014, the Nepal Samiti has been organizing large-scale events every year since 2015, in collaboration with the Indian Embassy in Kathmandu, local municipalities, and other national organizations.

These celebrations feature mass yoga demonstrations led by renowned Patanjali teachers and attract widespread public participation as well as high-profile dignitaries and spiritual leaders from both Nepal and India, highlighting the cultural and diplomatic importance of the shared yogic tradition. Patanjali leverages this platform to promote holistic health through yoga and Ayurveda.

A prominent example of these grand affairs was the 2019 celebration held at the historic Janki Mandir in Janakpur. The event underscored its national and international importance with the attendance of several esteemed guests, including the Honorable Deputy Prime Minister Mr. Upendra Yadav; the Honorable Chief Minister of Province No. 2, Mr. Mohammad Lalbabu Raut Gaddi; the Honorable Governor of Province No. 2, Mr. Ratneshwar Lal Kayastha; and India's Ambassador to Nepal, Mr. Manjit Singh Puri. The event also saw robust political and security representation from several MLAs and top officials from the security and army forces.

Fortified by the unwavering support of influential leaders and community figures, Patanjali Yog Samiti remains steadfast in its mission to disseminate the profound benefits of yoga, broadening its outreach each year to cultivate an environment of health, wellness, and unity across diverse populations.

Festival

Ram Navami

राम नवमी

Ram Navami is a sacred Hindu festival that commemorates the divine birth of Lord Rama, the seventh incarnation of Vishnu, on the ninth day of the Chaitra Navratri. This auspicious occasion celebrates righteousness, duty and virtue as embodied by Lord Rama, and symbolizes the eternal triumph of good over evil. His life, as recounted in the epic Ramayana, offers timeless lessons on how to lead a principled and moral life.

This sacred day also holds special significance for followers of the Patanjali tradition, marking the sannyas divas (renunciation day) of the revered Yoga Guru, Swami Ramdev Ji Maharaj. The occasion inspires spiritual aspirants to embrace a life of discipline and devotion, reinforcing the festival's themes of spiritual awakening and inner righteousness.

In Nepal, the celebrations extend beyond traditional worship. Devotees participate in the ancient Agnihotra ritual in yoga classes, a fire ceremony that purifies the atmosphere and reinforces spiritual dedication. This practice is complemented by compassionate community service, where people engage in social activities such as donating food and clothes to those in need — embodying the virtues of selfless service and compassion that Lord Rama exemplified.

Observance

Women’s Day

Patanjali Yog Samiti's annual celebration of International Women's Day is a heartfelt tribute to the extraordinary contributions of women in society and within the organization itself. Every year on March 8, women volunteers at the local level organize a special event that recognizes those who have paved the way for societal progress and the devoted women who tirelessly promote the principles of Yoga and Ayurveda.

During the event, women who have significantly impacted the community are publicly recognized and celebrated, receiving accolades for their inspiring work. Honoring these local heroes serves as a powerful source of motivation for other women and girls, and highlights the crucial role women play in the holistic wellness movement championed by Patanjali.

The celebration also functions as a unifying event, bringing together women from diverse backgrounds to share their experiences and strengthen their resolve — reinforcing a collective commitment to personal and communal well-being, and the transformative power of women's leadership in shaping a healthier, more balanced society.

Festival

Guru Purnima

गुरु पूर्णिमा

Patanjali Yogpeeth Nepal joyfully observes Guru Purnima, paying heartfelt tribute to its revered founders, Param Pujya Swami Ramdev Ji Maharaj and Param Shraddheya Acharya Balkrishna Ji Maharaj.

This sacred occasion, deeply rooted in the Vedic tradition, celebrates the timeless values of devotion, gratitude, and the eternal Guru-disciple bond. It serves as a reminder of the guiding light that spiritual masters bring into the lives of seekers, illuminating their journey towards knowledge, wisdom and self-realization.

In Nepal, Guru Purnima is celebrated with profound reverence and spiritual fervor. On this auspicious day, the dedicated volunteers of Patanjali Yoga Samiti, Mahila Patanjali Yoga Samiti, and Yuva Nepal Samiti come together to perform sacred Agnihotra rituals, conduct spiritual assemblies, and engage in selfless social service activities, offering their unwavering devotion and respect to their revered Gurus.

Observance

Jadi Buti Divas

Patanjali proudly celebrates August 4th — the birthday of Pujya Acharya Balkrishna Ji Maharaj — as "Jadi Buti Diwas" across the globe. On this auspicious day, millions of Patanjali volunteers come together to honor nature through a grand movement of medicinal plant and herb plantation, adding millions of trees to the earth every year.

This occasion is not only a celebration of Ayurveda and nature's healing power but also a moment of deep spiritual awakening. Devotees engage in Gayatri Mantra chanting and perform Gayatri Agnihotra Yajnas across countless locations, transforming the environment with vibrations of purity, devotion, and positive energy.

Through Jadi Buti Diwas, Patanjali continues to spread the message of harmony between nature, spirituality and humanity, inspired by the vision of Pujya Swami Ramdev Ji Maharaj and Pujya Acharya Balkrishna Ji Maharaj.

Festival

Haritalika Teej

हरितालिका तीज

Haritalika Teej is one of the most cherished festivals in Hindu tradition, celebrated by women in devotion to Goddess Parvati and her divine union with Lord Shiva. The festival symbolizes marital bliss, the well-being of one's spouse, and spiritual purification through fasting and prayer. Women come together to sing traditional folk songs, dance, and strengthen social and cultural bonds, making Teej a vibrant expression of faith, joy and togetherness.

Patanjali has played a pivotal role in promoting and preserving the cultural and spiritual essence of Haritalika Teej — raising awareness about its significance, engaging the Nepali diaspora, and helping transform Teej into a celebration observed on a global stage.

Patanjali Yogpeeth Nepal is deeply involved in celebrating Haritalika Teej with the Nepali community, highlighting the spiritual and cultural bonds between India and Nepal. Each year, the Patanjali family and its social wings organize grand celebrations that bring together people from both nations, showcasing unity, devotion and cultural pride.

Milestone

Sthapna Diwas

स्थापना दिवस

Patanjali Yogpeeth Nepal, registered as a charitable trust in 2006, was founded by Yogrishi Swami Ramdev Ji Maharaj and Acharya Balkrishna Ji Maharaj with the mission of promoting yoga and healthy living across Nepal. The organization conducts free yoga classes and camps in all 77 districts, develops herbal gardens, and trains Ayurvedic professionals.

Following the 7.8-magnitude earthquake in 2015, Patanjali led relief efforts worth ₹5 crore, including ₹1 crore in medicines. Volunteers set up 30 relief camps, mobilized over 30,000 people across 20+ districts, distributed 1,00,000+ food packets, 50,000 blankets and 10,000 tents, and provided over 10,000 meals daily during peak relief operations.

Patanjali has also adopted over 100 orphaned children, providing shelter, food and education, and helped end the 400-year-old animal sacrifice tradition at Nepal's Kalika Temple, saving more than 30,000 animals annually. The organization has conducted 38,208 free yoga and wellness camps reaching over 27,431 people, and its Ayurveda Medical College in Dhulikhel, established in 2016, offers a 5.5-year BAMS program affiliated with Nepal Sanskrit University, enrolling 40 students annually.

Campaign

Blood Donation Camp

Patanjali Yogpeeth Nepal views blood donation as a profound act of selfless service — a sacred offering that can bridge the gap between life and death. It is an act of pure compassion that offers healing and hope to those in critical need, a testament to the life-affirming power of human solidarity.

Drawing inspiration from the compassionate leadership of Param Shraddheya Acharya Balkrishna Ji Maharaj, who personally donates blood on his birthday and has led large-scale donation camps, volunteers across Patanjali Yogpeeth Nepal have been moved to carry that same commitment forward.

With steadfast commitment, these volunteers continuously organize blood donation camps throughout Nepal, ensuring the availability of life-saving blood for emergencies and vital medical care — reflecting Patanjali's vision of a healthier, more compassionate world.

Campaign

Swachchta Abhiyan

Patanjali's Swachchta Abhiyan is an expansive public health and sanitation campaign operating across Nepal and India, working in harmony with national missions like the Swachh Bharat Mission. Beyond simple cleaning efforts, it is a holistic, community-driven program designed to foster a deep-seated culture of cleanliness and hygiene.

The effort mobilizes volunteers from Patanjali's various organizations and educational institutions to carry out cleanliness drives in public spaces, educational facilities, and local communities. At its core, the campaign is grounded in education and awareness, encouraging behavior change and proper sanitation practices through outreach programs and direct engagement.

By engaging local residents, students and employees, the program transforms a broad national objective into a decentralized, people-powered movement — a catalyst for a cleaner, healthier society where cleanliness becomes a collective, ingrained way of life.

Campaign

Disaster Management & Relief Efforts

Nepal faces a high risk of natural disasters — earthquakes, landslides, floods and glacial lake outburst floods — due to its geography and geology, a risk compounded by deforestation and poor agricultural practices. Patanjali has been actively involved in disaster relief across Nepal, providing essential supplies, medical assistance and financial aid to affected areas.

Following the devastating earthquake that struck Kathmandu on 10 Baisakh 2072 BS, Patanjali established relief camps providing food, water, medical aid and shelter to thousands of survivors. Param Pujya Swami Ramdev Ji Maharaj and Param Shraddheya Acharya Balkrishna Ji Maharaj personally oversaw the distribution of approximately Rs. 70 million worth of aid, with Ayurvedic doctors, volunteers and yoga teachers offering health services and emotional support through yoga and meditation.

Patanjali continued its humanitarian efforts after another earthquake on 17 Kartik 2080 BS, assisting around 3,000 families with aid worth approximately Rs. 20 million, and most recently provided Rs. 20 million in relief to victims of floods and landslides in Bagmati Province in Bhadra 2081 BS. Alongside immediate assistance, Patanjali supports long-term preparedness, coordinating regularly with local bodies, health centers and community organizations on disaster mitigation and rehabilitation.
