प्राणायाम

The quiet discipline of conscious breath

Pranayama is the branch of yoga devoted to the breath — lengthening it, directing it, and occasionally suspending it, so that the mind that rides on it grows steady in turn. This is a working guide to the classical eight-step sequence, practised in the order given below, moving from the most active breaths to the most still.

What is pranayama?

Pranayama comes from two Sanskrit roots — prana, the vital breath or life force, and ayama, to extend or regulate. In the classical texts it is the fourth limb of Ashtanga Yoga, practised after posture has made the body steady enough to sit through longer breathing work.

Each technique below manages the breath differently: some slow it down, some speed it up, some route it through one nostril at a time. They are presented here as a single eight-step sequence, in the order they are traditionally practised together, moving from the more active breaths toward stillness and silent meditation.

Before you begin

  • Sit with the spine upright, on an empty stomach, ideally in the morning.
  • Keep the breath unforced. Strain, dizziness, or breathlessness is the signal to stop and rest.
  • The gentler breaths suit daily practice; the forceful ones are best learned in person from a teacher.
  • Ask a doctor or teacher first if you are pregnant, or managing a heart, lung, or blood-pressure condition.

8 techniques, in sequence

1. Bhastrika Pranayama

Bellows breath

भस्त्रिका

Named after a blacksmith’s bellows, this breath is active on both the inhale and the exhale, pumped through the nose at a steady, vigorous pace to build internal heat. It is traditionally the first practice in the sequence, warming the body before the others.

How it’s done

  1. Sit with the spine straight and take a few normal breaths.
  2. Inhale deeply and forcefully through both nostrils, expanding the chest.
  3. Exhale with equal force, pulling the abdomen in firmly.
  4. Continue this active inhale-exhale rhythm at a steady, vigorous pace.
  5. After a short round, return to normal breathing before moving on.

What it helps with

Energises and warms the body Traditionally builds vitality

Take care

A forceful practice. Beginners should start with very short rounds; avoid during pregnancy or with high blood pressure or a heart condition.

2. Kapalbhati Pranayama

Skull-shining breath

कपालभाति

A fast, forceful exhale from the lower abdomen followed by a passive inhale, repeated in quick succession. It follows Bhastrika in the sequence, continuing the work of clearing and activating.

How it’s done

  1. Sit tall with the hands resting on the knees.
  2. Take one normal breath in.
  3. Exhale sharply through the nose by pulling the navel firmly toward the spine.
  4. Let the inhale happen on its own, passively, between exhales.
  5. Continue in quick succession — begin with around 20 rounds and build up gradually with practice.

What it helps with

Energises the body Activates the abdominal muscles Supports respiratory clearing

Take care

A forceful practice. Avoid during pregnancy, or with high blood pressure or a hernia; learn it under guidance before practising alone.

3. Bahya Pranayama

External breath-retention

बाह्य प्राणायाम

A gentle breath retention taken after a full exhale, with the lungs kept empty for a comfortable pause. Coming third in the sequence, it follows the two activating breaths and is practised with a light chin lock.

How it’s done

  1. Sit tall and exhale completely through the nose.
  2. With the lungs empty, gently draw the abdomen in and tuck the chin lightly toward the chest.
  3. Hold this pause for a comfortable few seconds, without straining.
  4. Release the chin, relax the abdomen, and inhale slowly.
  5. Return to normal breathing before repeating for a few rounds.

What it helps with

Draws attention inward Settles the mind after activation

Take care

A breath-retention practice. Learn it under guidance, hold only as long as is comfortable, and avoid it during pregnancy or with high blood pressure.

4. Ujjayi Pranayama

Victorious breath

उज्जायी

A slow, audible breath made by gently narrowing the back of the throat, producing a soft sound often compared to ocean waves. Fourth in the sequence, it eases the pace down from the forceful breaths before it.

How it’s done

  1. Sit or lie down and let the throat relax.
  2. Inhale through the nose while softly constricting the back of the throat, creating a gentle rasping sound.
  3. Exhale through the nose, keeping the same soft constriction and sound.
  4. Keep the sound quiet — audible to you, not the room — and continue for the desired duration.

What it helps with

Builds steady focus Generates gentle internal heat Pairs naturally with asana

5. Anulom Vilom

Alternate nostril breathing

अनुलोम विलोम

Air is drawn in and released through one nostril at a time, without any pause between the two sides. Fifth in the sequence, it balances and evens out the breath after the more active practices.

How it’s done

  1. Sit with the spine upright and shoulders relaxed.
  2. Fold the index and middle fingers of the right hand into the palm, keeping the thumb and ring finger free.
  3. Close the right nostril with the thumb and inhale slowly through the left.
  4. Close the left nostril with the ring finger, release the thumb, and exhale through the right.
  5. Inhale through the right, then switch and exhale through the left to complete one round.
  6. Continue for 5–10 minutes, keeping the breath even and unforced.

What it helps with

Calms the mind Improves breathing balance Eases everyday stress

6. Bhramari Pranayama

Humming bee breath

भ्रामरी

Named for the low, steady hum of a bee, this practice is felt as much as it is heard — the vibration travels through the jaw and skull and settles the nervous system almost immediately. Sixth in the sequence, it turns attention further inward, toward the final two practices.

How it’s done

  1. Sit comfortably and close the eyes.
  2. Rest the index fingers lightly on the cartilage in front of each ear, or simply rest the hands on the knees.
  3. Inhale deeply through the nose.
  4. Exhale slowly while humming steadily with the mouth closed.
  5. Let the sound stay soft and even; repeat for several rounds.

What it helps with

Deep relaxation Releases tension Soothes the nervous system

7. Udgeeth Pranayama

Chanting Om breath

उद्गीथ प्राणायाम

A slow breath paired with the long, drawn-out chanting of “Om” on the exhale. Seventh in the sequence, its sound and vibration quiet the mind and prepare it for silent meditation.

How it’s done

  1. Sit comfortably with the eyes closed and the spine upright.
  2. Inhale slowly and deeply through the nose.
  3. Exhale slowly while chanting “Om” aloud, letting the sound stretch out for as long as the breath allows.
  4. Let the hum of the “mmm” linger gently at the end of each exhale.
  5. Continue for several rounds, keeping the chant unhurried and even.

What it helps with

Quiets mental chatter Prepares the mind for meditation

8. Pranav Dhyan

Om meditation

प्रणव ध्यान

A closing, silent meditation on the sound and stillness of “Om”, done without any deliberate breath control. The final step in the sequence, it lets the effects of the seven breaths that came before settle into quiet.

How it’s done

  1. Sit in a steady, comfortable meditative posture with the eyes closed.
  2. Let the breath return to its own natural rhythm, without controlling it.
  3. Bring attention to the silent repetition of “Om” in the mind, or simply to the stillness that follows the chanting breath before it.
  4. Let thoughts arise and pass without engaging them, returning attention gently each time it wanders.
  5. Sit for several minutes before opening the eyes slowly.

What it helps with

Deepens stillness after the breathing practices Closes the sequence with quiet awareness
